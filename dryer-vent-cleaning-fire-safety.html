Dryer Vent Cleaning: Why Lint Build-Up Is a Fire Risk

Why dryer vents clog, the warning signs, what owners can do themselves and when a professional cleaning makes sense.

The leading cause of dryer fires

The U.S. Fire Administration says failure to clean the dryer is the leading cause of home clothes dryer fires. Lint slips past the screen and collects in the vent pipe, where hot air keeps passing over it.

Signs a vent needs cleaning

  • Clothes take longer than one cycle to dry
  • The outside of the dryer or the clothes feel very hot
  • A burning smell when the dryer runs
  • The flap on the outside vent hood barely opens when the dryer is on
  • Lint or debris around the outside vent opening

What owners can do

  • Clean the lint screen before or after every load
  • Use rigid or semi-rigid metal vent pipe, not plastic or thin foil
  • Keep the vent run as short and straight as the space allows
  • Check that the outdoor hood opens and is not blocked by nests or plants

When to call a professional

Long vent runs, roof vents and runs with several turns are hard to clean well from the laundry room. A professional cleaning uses rotary brushes and a vacuum to clear the full length and checks the airflow afterward. Questions people ask

How often should a dryer vent be cleaned?
Many fire safety guides suggest a yearly check, with more frequent cleaning for busy laundry rooms and long vent runs.
Is a dryer vent the same as an air duct?
No. The dryer vent is a separate pipe that carries hot, damp air and lint outside. It is not part of the heating and cooling ducts, though many companies clean both.
