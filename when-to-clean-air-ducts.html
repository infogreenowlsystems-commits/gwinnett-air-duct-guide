When Air Duct Cleaning Makes Sense, and What a Proper Job Includes

The times the EPA says duct cleaning is worth considering, what a full cleaning covers, and what to ask before booking.

What the EPA says

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency does not recommend cleaning air ducts on a routine schedule, and it notes that duct cleaning has not been shown to prevent health problems. It does name the times cleaning is worth considering:

  • Substantial mold growth that can be seen inside hard-surface ducts or other parts of the heating and cooling system
  • Ducts infested with insects or rodents
  • Ducts clogged with too much dust and debris, or particles blowing out of the supply registers into the home

Many homeowners also have the system checked after a remodel that sent drywall dust through the vents, or when they move into a home whose history they do not know.

What a proper cleaning includes

The industry standard most often cited is the National Air Duct Cleaners Association's ACR standard, which covers assessing, cleaning and restoring heating and cooling systems. In plain terms, a proper job:

  • Puts the system under negative pressure with a strong vacuum, so loosened dust is pulled out instead of blown into the house
  • Uses brushes, air whips or other tools to break debris loose from the duct walls
  • Cleans the whole path the air takes: supply and return ducts, registers and grilles, the blower and its housing, the coils and the drain pan
  • Shows the owner before and after photos or camera footage

Questions to ask before booking

  • Will the whole system be cleaned, including the air handler, or only the vents?
  • Is the crew NADCA certified?
  • Will any chemical treatment be used, and is it registered with the EPA for use in ducts?
  • Can I see before and after photos?

The EPA advises owners to understand any chemical biocide or sealant before agreeing to it, and to avoid companies that make sweeping health claims or push duct cleaning as routine maintenance.

Questions people ask

How often should air ducts be cleaned?
The EPA sets no schedule. The usual advice is to clean when one of the signs above shows up, and to have the system looked at when buying a home or after a remodel.
Will cleaning the ducts lower energy bills?
The EPA says there is little evidence that cleaning only the ductwork improves how well a system runs. Dirty coils, fans and filters are a different story; cleaning those parts can help the system work as designed.
Does a good filter make duct cleaning unnecessary?
A good filter, changed on time, keeps much of the dust out of the ducts in the first place. It does not remove mold, pests or debris already inside.
